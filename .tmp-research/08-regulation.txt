Here are the top crypto regulation and policy developments from the week of March 23-29, 2026:

### 1. The SEC & CFTC Issue Joint "Watershed" Crypto Guidance
While officially released on March 17, the **"Application of the Federal Securities Laws to Crypto Assets"** interpretive release dominated legal analysis and market sentiment throughout this week. The agencies established a clear five-category taxonomy for digital assets:
*   **Digital Commodities** (e.g., BTC), regulated by the CFTC.
*   **Payment Stablecoins**, regulated under the newly enacted GENIUS Act.
*   **Digital Collectibles** (NFTs) and **Digital Tools** (utility tokens), which generally fall outside securities laws.
*   **Digital Securities**, which explicitly meet the *Howey* test.

Notably, SEC Chairman Paul Atkins formally acknowledged that "most crypto assets are not themselves securities," signaling a definitive end to the "regulation by enforcement" era. The guidance also created explicit safe harbors for protocol-level mining, staking, and no-consideration airdrops.

### 2. Bipartisan Breakthrough on the "CLARITY Act"
On March 20, Senators Thom Tillis and Angela Alsobrooks announced an "agreement in principle" with the White House regarding the **Digital Asset Market Clarity (CLARITY) Act**. This legislation specifically addresses the controversial regulatory treatment of stablecoin yield, aiming to allow regulated yield-bearing crypto products while instituting guardrails to protect traditional bank deposits. Despite the breakthrough, analysts noted lingering "legal gridlock" in the Senate as a short-term hurdle.

### 3. Progress on Stablecoin Regulation (The GENIUS Act)
Regulators pushed forward with the implementation of the **Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins (GENIUS) Act**. On March 24, the Treasury Department issued new licensing guidelines specifically tailored for non-bank stablecoin issuers. On the same day, Delaware introduced bipartisan state-level legislation to create a stablecoin licensing regime mirroring the GENIUS Act's federal definitions. 

### 4. SEC Approves TradFi Tokenization Initiatives
Significant traditional finance (TradFi) integration took place under explicit SEC approval. Nasdaq secured a rule change to enable the trading of tokenized securities as part of a DTC pilot program. Concurrently, on March 24, the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) announced a partnership with Securitize to launch a **Digital Trading Platform**, enabling 24/7 trading and on-chain stablecoin settlement for tokenized U.S. equities and ETFs.
