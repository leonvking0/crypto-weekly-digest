Here are the most important stablecoin news items for the week of March 23-29, 2026, focusing on insights from The Block and Messari, as well as major developments for USDT and USDC:

1. **Tether (USDT) Engages KPMG for its First Full Audit**
According to reporting highlighted by *The Block*, Tether has made a historic move by hiring "Big Four" accounting firm KPMG to conduct its first-ever full independent financial statement audit, moving away from its previous "point-in-time" attestations by BDO Italia. PwC was also reportedly brought in to help prepare Tether's internal systems for the rigorous scrutiny. USDT remains dominant with a massive $184 billion market cap, though its liquidity growth flattened this week. 

2. **Messari Predicts "Yield-Bearing" Shift in "Crypto Theses 2026"**
*Messari's* latest research reports emphasize a massive structural transition in the stablecoin market. According to Messari, stablecoins are shifting from "passive settlement tools" to "active yield-generating instruments." The firm predicts that yield-bearing stablecoins will rapidly erode the market share of traditional, non-yielding assets like standard USDC and USDT as institutional players demand a "risk-free" ~5% return on-chain.

3. **Regulatory Impasse: The Block Reports on the Stalled "Clarity Act"**
*The Block* has been closely tracking the legislative gridlock in Washington regarding the "Clarity Act." The legislation has stalled primarily over how to treat stablecoin rewards and yield generation. This regulatory uncertainty caused Circle's stock (CRCL) to drop mid-month. Meanwhile, lawmakers are also debating the "GENIUS Act," which seeks to integrate stablecoins more formally into the U.S. monetary framework.

4. **Circle (USDC) Expands to Consumer Mortgages**
In a major utility milestone, Circle announced a partnership with Coinbase and Better Home & Finance to allow USDC to be used as collateral for U.S. home loans backed by Fannie Mae. This marks a significant integration of stablecoins into core traditional consumer finance. 

5. **USDC Supply Contraction and Wallet Freeze Controversy**
It was a turbulent week for USDC's supply and PR. USDC saw a net reduction of $1.1 billion in circulation over the last week of March. Additionally, on March 23, Circle controversially froze 16 business hot wallets (linked to exchanges and forex platforms) due to a sealed U.S. civil lawsuit. Facing intense community backlash, Circle began unfreezing several of the wallets by March 26. Despite this, early March data showed USDC briefly flipping USDT in adjusted transaction volume for the first time since early 2026.
